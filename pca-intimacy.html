Intimacy Counselling

Pain shouldn’t get the final say in how you connect.

Chronic pain can affect physical intimacy in ways that aren’t always easy to talk about. Certain movements may become uncomfortable, and fatigue can leave you with little energy for closeness. Pain can also affect desire — medication, stress, exhaustion, and the emotional toll of persistent pain can make intimacy feel very different from how it used to.

  • A supportive space to explore changes in closeness and desire
  • Attend individually or with your partner
  • 100% telehealth — private sessions from home

You don’t have to let pain write the rules for your relationship.

When Pain Gets Between You

Sometimes the hardest part of chronic pain isn’t the pain itself.

You may avoid physical closeness because certain movements are uncomfortable, or because you’re worried about triggering a flare. You may also be too exhausted to feel interested in intimacy at all.

And when these changes continue over time, they can affect both partners. Frustration, rejection, misunderstanding, and uncertainty can gradually create distance in even a loving relationship. That doesn’t mean you have stopped caring about each other — it means chronic pain has introduced challenges that the two of you may need new ways of navigating.

How It Helps

How Intimacy Counselling Can Help

Intimacy counselling can help you and your partner navigate the relationship challenges that chronic pain can bring. Depending on your needs, it may help you:

1.

Adapt to physical limitations

Explore ways of maintaining physical closeness when certain movements or activities have become uncomfortable.

2.

Navigate changes in desire

Understand and communicate about changes in desire without turning them into blame, pressure, or self-doubt.

3.

Rebuild confidence

Work through body-image concerns and changes in physical confidence that may affect your willingness to be close.

4.

Communicate your needs

Have more open conversations about comfort, boundaries, energy, expectations, and what feels right for both partners.

5.

Work through relationship strain

Address the frustration, distance, or misunderstanding that can develop when chronic pain begins affecting a couple’s connection.

6.

Discover new ways to connect

Find practical approaches to intimacy that take your current body, energy, comfort, and circumstances into account.

What to Expect

What Happens During Intimacy Counselling?

Your sessions are tailored to your relationship, circumstances, and comfort level. We work with you to understand how chronic pain is affecting your connection and explore practical ways to address it.

How pain affects physical closeness Changes in desire or libido Fatigue and its effect on relationships Body image and confidence Communication between partners Comfort, positioning, pacing and limitations Maintaining connection during difficult periods Adapting intimacy around changing energy levels

There is no expectation to perform, prove anything, or have all the answers. The goal is a supportive space where you can talk openly about parts of your relationship that may be difficult to discuss elsewhere.
