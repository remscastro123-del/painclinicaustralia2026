Chronic Pain: What It Is, Why It Happens & How to Find Relief

Pain Clinic Australia / June 2, 2026 / Chronic Pain

What Is Chronic Pain?

Pain is the body's built-in alarm system. In most situations, it's a helpful signal that something needs attention, and it fades once the underlying cause heals. That kind of short-term pain is called acute pain.

Chronic pain is something different altogether. It's pain that persists for three months or longer, often continuing well after any original injury has healed, or sometimes occurring with no clear injury at all. Rather than serving as a useful warning, chronic pain becomes its own condition — one that reshapes the nervous system, disrupts sleep, affects mood, and touches every corner of daily life.

The International Association for the Study of Pain (IASP) classifies chronic pain into two main categories. Primary chronic pain is where pain itself is the central problem — conditions like fibromyalgia or chronic widespread pain. Secondary chronic pain arises as a symptom of another condition, such as arthritis, endometriosis, or cancer.

Despite how common it is — affecting over 3.2 million Australians — chronic pain remains one of the most under-treated conditions in our healthcare system. Many patients are told to simply live with it, or find that standard pain medications stop working over time. The reality is that modern pain management has moved well beyond prescriptions alone.

  • Chronic pain affects more than 1 in 5 Australians at some point in their lives
  • 68% of chronic pain patients are of working age, significantly impacting productivity and quality of life
  • Effective treatment almost always requires a combination of approaches tailored to the individual

Why Does Chronic Pain Persist?

One of the most important — and least understood — aspects of chronic pain is that it frequently persists not because of ongoing tissue damage, but because of changes in the nervous system itself. This is called central sensitisation: the brain and spinal cord become hypersensitive, amplifying pain signals even when the original injury has resolved.

This explains why someone can have a scan that looks structurally normal and still experience severe pain, why weather changes can trigger flares, and why stress, poor sleep, and emotional distress make chronic pain significantly worse. Pain is not simply a physical signal — it is deeply influenced by psychological, social, and biological factors all at once.

What Genuinely Helps?

Modern evidence strongly supports a multidisciplinary approach — combining medical care, physical rehabilitation, psychological support, and nutritional guidance. No single treatment works for everyone, but coordinated specialist care consistently produces better outcomes than any one modality alone.

At Pain Clinic Australia, our treatment plans bring together licensed pain doctors, APHRA-registered physiotherapists, evidence-based psychologists, and clinical nutritionists. A dedicated case manager coordinates your care and checks in monthly — because chronic pain management is a journey, not a single appointment.
